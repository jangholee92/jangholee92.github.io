Climate hazards become consequential through their effects on people and infrastructure. I link observations and climate projections with demographic, health, and energy data to quantify those effects. A mortality analysis across 106 U.S. cities separates the roles of warming, population change, and adaptation; work on the Texas grid shows how relying on recent weather alone can understate peak electricity demand. Analysis of heat-related searches across 30 metropolitan areas also reveals how local climate and social vulnerability shape public awareness.

At neighborhood scale, I examine how rainfall, infrastructure, and social conditions influence reported flooding in Chicago. I also study how weather, air quality, and future warming affect bike-sharing across five U.S. cities, providing evidence for resilient, low-carbon travel. Together, these studies inform targeted heat communication, equitable flood adaptation, climate-aware energy planning, and mitigation strategies that remain useful as conditions change.
